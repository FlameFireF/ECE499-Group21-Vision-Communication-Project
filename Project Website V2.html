Background About the Project

Communication is an important part of daily life, education, employment, and social interaction. However, people who rely on sign language or other non-verbal communication methods may experience barriers when interacting with individuals who do not understand these forms of communication. This can create challenges in public services, workplaces, classrooms, and everyday conversations.

The social issue addressed by this project is the accessibility gap between people who use visual or gesture-based communication and people who are unfamiliar with it. A portable recognition system could help reduce communication barriers by detecting hand gestures or signs and converting them into readable or audible output.

  • Improves accessibility for people who rely on visual communication.
  • Supports faster communication between users and non-signers.
  • Encourages inclusive technology design.
  • Demonstrates how embedded systems and computer vision can be used for social benefit.

Design

The team addressed the problem by designing a camera-based recognition system that captures visual input, processes the image data, identifies predefined gestures or signs, and provides an output to the user. The system is intended to be portable, low-cost, and simple enough to demonstrate as an engineering prototype.

Input Stage

A camera module captures images or video frames of the user’s hand gestures. Proper lighting, camera angle, and background conditions are considered to improve recognition reliability.

Processing Stage

The captured images are processed by a microcontroller or external computer. Image processing and machine learning techniques are used to classify the gesture or sign.

Output Stage

After recognition, the system displays the result as text and may also provide audio output using a speaker, audio amplifier, or text-to-speech module.

The prototype design may include components such as an ESP32-CAM or similar camera module, a microcontroller, a speaker or audio module, a power supply, and software for gesture recognition. The system is tested using a limited set of signs or gestures to evaluate recognition accuracy and response time.

Results / Findings

The project demonstrated that a camera-based recognition system can be used to identify selected human communication gestures. Testing helped the team understand the strengths and limitations of the prototype, especially in relation to lighting, background conditions, hand position, processing speed, and dataset quality.

Recognition Performance

The prototype was able to recognize a selected group of gestures during controlled testing. Accuracy improved when the hand was clearly visible and the background was simple.

System Limitations

Recognition performance decreased when lighting was poor, the background was complex, or the user’s hand position was inconsistent. More training data would be needed for better real-world performance.

Future Improvements

Future work could include a larger sign language dataset, improved machine learning models, better audio output, a more compact enclosure, and real-time testing with more users.
